Punctures

Got a puncture? Here’s what to do.

A puncture on a bike is more dangerous than in a car, and riding on a flat tyre can wreck the tyre and the rim. Here’s how to decide what to do.

First, stop safely

If the bike suddenly feels vague, weaves or wobbles, roll off the throttle gently, don’t brake hard, and steer smoothly to a safe stop away from traffic. A deflating front tyre is particularly dangerous; don’t try to ride on to the next town.

Can you plug it?

A tubeless tyre with a small puncture in the centre of the tread can often be plugged with a roadside kit, then inflated with CO2 cartridges or a small pump. Ride slowly and directly to a tyre fitter. A plug is a get-you-home repair; many fitters will then replace the tyre.

Don’t plug it if:

  • The hole is in the sidewall or near the edge of the tread.
  • It’s a tear or a cut rather than a small hole.
  • The tyre has a tube in it (most spoked wheels and older bikes).
  • You’ve ridden on it flat and the tyre or rim is damaged.

Foam sealants

Aerosol sealants can get you going, but they make a mess of the inside of the tyre and some fitters won’t repair a tyre afterwards. Tell the fitter you used one.

When to call us

If you can’t plug it, it’s a front tyre you don’t trust, or you just don’t want to risk it, call us. We’ll take the bike to a tyre fitter, a dealer or home. See breakdown recovery.

FAQ

Rider questions

Can I ride on a plugged tyre?

Slowly and for a short distance, to get to a tyre fitter. Treat it as temporary.

Can you take my bike straight to a tyre fitter?

Yes, if it’s open. Otherwise home or a dealer.
