After a crash

Had a motorbike accident? What to do, in order.

Adrenaline hides injuries and scrambles memory. Work through this list, and leave the bike for last.

  3. What to do after a motorbike crash
  1. Get safe. Out of the road, behind a barrier or onto a verge. Warn traffic only if it’s safe to.
  2. Check for injuries. Call 999 if anyone is hurt. Don’t remove a helmet if there’s neck or back pain. Let paramedics do it.
  3. Call the police if anyone is injured, the road is blocked, the other driver leaves, or you suspect drink or drugs.
  4. Swap details: names, addresses, phone numbers, registrations and insurers. Get witness contacts too.
  5. Collect evidence. Photos of the bike, the vehicles, the road surface (diesel, gravel, potholes), signs and the view from where you were. Helmet-camera footage is gold, so save it straight away.
  6. Get the bike moved. Once the police are done, call us. We’ll recover it to your home, a dealer or storage. See accident recovery.
  7. Tell your insurer as soon as you can, even if you’re not claiming.
  8. Get checked by a doctor within a day or two, even if you feel fine. Some injuries show up later.

What the law says you must do

If you’re involved in a collision on a road in Great Britain that injures someone, or damages another vehicle or someone’s property, you must stop. Anyone with reasonable grounds can ask for your name and address, the bike owner’s name and address, and the registration, and you must give them. If someone is injured, you must also show your insurance.

If you can’t exchange details at the scene, for example because the other driver has gone or the owner of a damaged parked car can’t be found, report the accident to the police as soon as you reasonably can, and within 24 hours at the latest.

Knocked off by a car?

Many bike crashes happen at junctions, when a driver pulls out or turns across a rider they didn’t see. Don’t argue about blame at the roadside, and don’t apologise in a way that sounds like admitting fault. Just collect the facts: where the car was, whether it was signalling, the speed limit, the weather and the road surface. Ask witnesses for a phone number before they drive off, because they’re hard to find later. If it was a company van, photograph the livery and any fleet number.

What happens to your bike

If the police close the road, they may call their own contracted recovery firm. A bike they remove goes to a storage yard, and yards charge for every day it stays there, so arrange collection quickly. If you’re free to choose, decide where it should go: your home, a dealer, a bodyshop your insurer approves, or secure storage. We can collect it from the roadside or from the yard. See accident recovery.

Don’t ride a crashed bike home, even if it starts. Bent bars, twisted forks or a cracked lever can fail at the next corner. Photograph the bike before it’s moved, and again once it’s off the truck.

Your insurer, a replacement bike and a claim

  • Tell your own insurer promptly, even if it wasn’t your fault and you don’t plan to claim on your policy. Most policies require it.
  • Replacement bike. If the other driver was at fault, you may be able to get a similar bike while yours is off the road, through their insurer or a credit hire company. Read the agreement before you sign: some leave you paying for the hire if the claim fails.
  • Injury claims. In England and Wales you normally have three years from the date of the accident to start an injury claim. The 2021 whiplash rules that cap small road-traffic injury claims don’t apply to motorcyclists or pillion passengers.
  • Keep everything: damaged kit, receipts, medical notes, time off work and travel costs to appointments.

This is general information, not legal advice. For an injury claim, speak to a solicitor who handles motorcycle cases.

Your kit

A helmet that has taken a hit should be replaced, even if it looks fine. Keep damaged kit and photograph it, because it’s part of your claim.

FAQ

Rider questions

Should I take my helmet off after a motorbike accident?

Not if you have any neck or back pain, numbness or tingling, or you were knocked out. Leave it on and let paramedics remove it. If you’re unhurt you can take it off, but keep it: a helmet that has taken a hit should be replaced.

Do I have to report a motorbike accident to the police?

You must report it within 24 hours if you couldn’t exchange details at the scene, or if someone was injured and you couldn’t show your insurance there and then. Call 999 straight away if anyone is hurt, the road is blocked, or a driver leaves the scene.

Can I ride my bike home after a crash?

Only if you’re unhurt and you’ve checked the bike properly: bars straight, forks not twisted, brakes and levers working, and no fuel or oil leaking. If in doubt, get it recovered.

How long do I have to claim after a motorbike accident?

In England and Wales, usually three years from the date of the accident for an injury claim. Don’t leave it that long: evidence and witnesses are much easier to find early on.
